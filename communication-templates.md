# Communication Templates

This reference provides templates and guidelines for drafting professional communications following a business investigation. All communications must be factual, concise, and action-oriented.

## General Communication Principles

- **Factual**: State what happened, what was found, and what is being done. No speculation, blame, or emotional language.
- **Concise**: Managers and external parties are busy. Lead with the bottom line, then provide detail.
- **Action-Oriented**: Every communication should make clear what the recipient needs to do, what is already being done, and by when.
- **Tone-Appropriate**: Match the tone to the audience and severity. Internal team communications can be direct. Supplier communications should be professional and collaborative. Management communications should be executive-level.
- **Confidence-Transparent**: State the confidence level of the root cause finding. Do not present uncertain conclusions as definitive.

## Template 1: Management Briefing Email

**Audience**: Senior management or business owner.
**Purpose**: Inform leadership of the investigation outcome and the action plan.
**Tone**: Executive, confident, action-oriented.

```
Subject: [URGENT/HIGH/MEDIUM] [Business Problem Summary] - Investigation Complete & Action Plan

Dear [Manager Name],

Investigation into [business problem, e.g., "the 34.8% revenue decline in October"] is complete.

Root Cause (Confidence: [HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW]):
[One to two sentence summary of the validated root cause, e.g., "Internal PO approval delays caused Product A stockouts for 33 hours, directly accounting for the revenue loss."]

Financial Impact:
- Total loss: [amount] ([percentage]% decline)
- Primary driver: [brief description]
- Projected monthly impact if uncorrected: [amount]

Immediate Actions Taken / Underway:
1. [Action 1 - with owner and deadline]
2. [Action 2 - with owner and deadline]
3. [Action 3 - with owner and deadline]

Preventive Measures Planned:
1. [Plan 1 - with owner and timeline]
2. [Plan 2 - with owner and timeline]

Monitoring:
[Key KPI] is now being tracked [daily/weekly] with a warning threshold at [value] and critical threshold at [value].

The full action register is attached. I will provide the next status update on [date].

Regards,
[Sender Name]
[Sender Role]
```

## Template 2: Supplier Communication

**Audience**: Supplier or vendor.
**Purpose**: Address a supply-side issue, request action, or communicate a process change.
**Tone**: Professional, collaborative, firm.

```
Subject: [Action Required / Process Update] [Specific Issue] - [Company Name]

Dear [Supplier Contact Name],

[Opening: State the purpose directly, e.g., "I am writing regarding the delivery delays on Product A observed in [month]."]

[Context: Briefly state the impact, e.g., "These delays resulted in stockouts that affected our sales performance. We value our partnership and want to work together to prevent recurrence."]

[Request/Action: State what is needed, e.g., "Could we schedule a call this week to discuss: (1) the cause of the recent delays, (2) a recovery plan for the current backlog, and (3) preventive measures including updated lead time commitments."]

[If process change: "Going forward, we request [specific change, e.g., "a 48-hour advance notification of any shipment delay exceeding 24 hours"] as part of our updated ordering process."]

Please confirm receipt and your availability for a discussion.

Best regards,
[Sender Name]
[Sender Role]
[Company Name]
```

## Template 3: Internal Team Memo

**Audience**: Internal team members (operations, sales, warehouse, etc.).
**Purpose**: Communicate the investigation findings, new procedures, and individual responsibilities.
**Tone**: Direct, clear, supportive.

```
Subject: [Action Required] [Issue Summary] - New Procedures & Responsibilities

Team,

[Summary: "As you may know, we experienced [problem, e.g., "a significant revenue drop last month due to stockouts on key products"]. We have completed an investigation and identified the root cause."]

Root Cause:
[One sentence summary.]

What We Found:
- [Finding 1]
- [Finding 2]
- [Finding 3]

What Changes Starting Today:
1. [New procedure 1, e.g., "PO approvals must be completed within 4 hours. If the primary approver is unavailable, escalate to [backup approver]."]
2. [New procedure 2]
3. [New procedure 3]

Your Responsibilities:
- [Role 1]: [Specific responsibility and deadline]
- [Role 2]: [Specific responsibility and deadline]

We will track the following KPI going forward: [KPI name] with a daily check. If it hits [warning threshold], notify [manager] immediately.

Questions? Contact [name/role].

[Sender Name]
[Sender Role]
```

## Template 4: Customer Communication (If Applicable)

**Audience**: Affected customers.
**Purpose**: Address service disruption, rebuild confidence, and communicate resolution.
**Tone**: Empathetic, professional, reassuring.

```
Subject: Update from [Company Name] - [Issue Reference]

Dear [Customer Name / Valued Customers],

[Acknowledge: "We are aware that [issue, e.g., "Product A was unavailable for a period last week"]. We sincerely apologize for any inconvenience this caused."]

[Explain briefly: "The issue was caused by [brief, non-technical explanation]. We have identified the root cause and taken immediate steps to resolve it."]

[Resolution: "Effective [date], [what has been fixed, e.g., "Product A is back in stock with replenishment secured for the coming weeks"]. To prevent this from happening again, we have [preventive measure summary]."]

[Goodwill gesture if applicable: "As a token of our commitment to your satisfaction, [offer]."]

Thank you for your patience and continued trust.

[Sender Name]
[Sender Role]
[Company Name]
```

## Customization Rules

- Replace all bracketed placeholders `[...]` with specific, factual content.
- Remove sections that do not apply to the specific situation.
- Add company-specific formatting (letterhead, signature blocks) as needed.
- Adapt the subject line urgency tag to match the action priority (URGENT for Critical, HIGH for High, etc.).
- For supplier communications, maintain a collaborative tone even when the supplier is at fault — the goal is prevention, not accusation.
- For customer communications, keep technical details minimal and focus on what was done and what changes.
